     Relevant facts for disposal of the matter, in brief, are that, the
petitioner joined as District Relief and Rehabilitation Officer on
13.02.1989 in the Department of the Disaster Management, under
the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief. Thereafter, he
was promoted to the post of ‘Deputy Director’ on 01.10.2006 and
he was given the current charge of the vacant post of ‘Director’ on
22.03.2009. Lastly, he was given promotion to the post of Director
on 22.10.2012. As per Rule 43(1)(a) of the Government
Employment Scheme, 2018 (Act No. 57 of 2018), the petitioner
was allowed to go on PRL on 09.09.2021 and he retired as
‘Director’ with effect from 09.09.2022. Earlier, vide memo dated
06.01.2019 issued by the Ministry of Finance, Finance Division all
pension granting authorities were directed to refrain from
unintended and illegal practice of non-payment of pension benefits
or delay in granting permission due to non-completion of audit
report of any period of service of the retired employees. But the
respondents refrained from disbursing petitioner’s pension benefits
as yet.
Being aggrieved thereby the petitioner served a Notice
Demanding Justice upon the respondents on 07.04.2025 praying for
disbursement of his lawful entitlement of pension benefits,
however, no response has been made thereto till date. Finding no
other alternative and equally efficacious remedy available, the

petitioner has filed the instant Writ Petition and obtained the
present Rule.
Ms. Amatul Karim with Mr. Md. Motiar Rahman, learned
Advocates appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that,
after completion of unblemished service the petitioner is lawfully
entitled to get his pension benefit in due course but the respondents
have been refrained from making the disbursement and verbally
denying the same without any valid reason in gross violation of
law.
Learned Advocate next submitted that, retirement benefit is a
property within the meaning of Article 152 of the Constitution of
Bangladesh and the Constitution provides that no person shall be
deprived of his property save and except by the authority of Law.
The duty of the state to disburse the pension immediately on the
retirement of an employee is obligatory. Withholding and denying
the pension benefits to the petitioner is liable to be declared illegal
and without any lawful basis.
She strongly submitted that, considering the Bangladesh
Service Rules (BSR) Part I, Rules 242-505, there is no statutory
basis for the sanctioning authority not to pay pension wages or
making any delay in processing the sanction of pension if any
period of service of the employee remains unaudited and as such
the respondents are required to be directed to pay the pension
benefits to the petitioner forthwith.

She forcefully submitted that, respondents have illegally
detained the money of the petitioner. After having served for 31
(thirty one) years, it was the bona fide and legitimate expectation of
the petitioner that he would be paid his entitled retirement benefits
immediately after his retirement. Despite submitting repeated
representations and service of legal notice upon the authority, it is
not disbursing the pension benefits to the petitioner in a mala fide
and in an arbitrary manner.
By filing a supplementary affidavit dated 22.05.2025 learned
Advocate for the petitioner further submitted that, there were as
many as 8 (eight) officials designated to the post of Deputy
Director, who worked under the petitioner's supervision and gone
on retirement, have been paid full pension benefits without any
objection. But the authority is withholding the lawful entitlement of
the petitioner in an unlawful manner.
She also submitted that, the petitioner applied for pension in
due process on 28.06.2022 and the department of Disaster
Management opined that there are no owing of the Department
from the petitioner and forwarded his documents to the Secretary,
Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief vide memo dated
21.08.2022. Hence, the respondents are lawfully obligated to make
payment of the petitioner’s pension benefits at once.
Learned Advocate finally submitted that, if there is any audit
objection during the service period of the petitioner, that should be
resolved within the specified period of 3 (three) months as per the

Public Servants’ Pension Simplification Order, 2020. That
stipulated period has already elapsed much earlier on 28.09.2022.
Therefore, the petitioner is entitled to get the full pension and
service benefits with interest for the willful delay committed by the
respondent authority. In support of her submission she placed
reliance on the case of A. J. Randhawa Supdg. Engineer (Retd.)
vs. State of Punjab and others reported in (1997) 117 PLR 6. In
this case the authority was directed to grant pension benefits with
interest because of the delay in making the lawful entitlement of the
petitioner.
Conversely, learned Advocates Mr. Sajed Sami Ahammad
with Mr. Md. Zakaria, entered appearance on behalf of the
respondent No. 2 i.e. the Director General (DG) of Department of
Disaster Management and Relief and contested the Rule by filing
an affidavit in opposition.
Learned Advocate submitted that, the petitioner served as a
Director in different sectors of the Department of the Disaster
Management, Dhaka. During his service, as many as 8 (eight) audit
objections were raised against him in respect of Taka 220 Crore in
yearly Audit Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of
Bangladesh (C&AG) and 2 (two) audit objections still remained
unresolved. For this reason his pension benefits are not being
disbursed. There is no willful inaction on behalf of the respondent
authority.

He further submitted that, the Broad Sheet (BS) reply to the
audit objections raised against the petitioner was sent several times
to Directorate of Audit. The tri-partite meetings on the audit
objections were held recommending for settlement of the same.
However, it was not accepted as being settled by the Directorate of
Social Safety and Audit.
He next submitted that, the Comptroller and Auditor General
of Bangladesh (C&AG), being empowered by the Constitution,
plays a crucial role in public sectors auditing, ensuring that the
public funds are spent in accordance with law. The audit
observations were raised against the petitioner during his service in
the C&AG's report and the same are remained unsettled. Hence, the
petitioner cannot get his retirement benefits until the audit
objections raised against him are fully settled.
          It is the contention of the learned Advocate that, the
respondents have taken necessary steps for settlement of the audit
observations raised against the petitioner by sending Broad Sheet
reply and holding tri-partite meetings on the audit objections. They
acted with utmost good faith. There is no mala fide intention or
arbitrariness on the part of the respondents in not paying the
retirement benefits to the petitioner and as such the Rule issued by
this Hon'ble Court may kindly be discharged.

We have heard the learned Advocates for the respective

Since the learned Advocate appearing for Respondent No. 2
has agitated the question of maintainability of the instant writ
petition, we deem it appropriate to address this issue at the outset.
The petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court
by way of a petition in the nature of Mandamus, seeking a direction
upon the respondents to disburse the pension benefits to which he is
lawfully entitled following his retirement from service. There is no
dispute either as to the petitioner's service or as to his entitlement to
the pension benefits. It is, however, evident that such benefits have
not yet been disbursed owing to an unresolved audit objection
before the Comptroller and Auditor General of Bangladesh.

It is pertinent to observe that the petitioner has not
approached this Court for the purpose of establishing or
determining his right to pension. Rather, he has invoked the writ
jurisdiction of this Court seeking a direction in the nature of
mandamus commanding the respondents to perform an act which
they are otherwise legally bound to perform.
In the circumstances, where the petitioner's entitlement is
undisputed and the respondents' obligation to discharge the
corresponding statutory duty is apparent, we are of the considered
view that the instant writ petition is maintainable in its present
form, being a proper invocation of the jurisdiction of this Court
under Article 102(2)(1)(i) of the Constitution of the People's
Republic of Bangladesh.
It is admitted that the petitioner went on Post Retirement
Leave (PRL) on 09.09.2021 and he was supposed to go on
retirement on completion of one year i.e. on 09.09.2022.
Accordingly on 28.06.2022 he made a representation before the
authority praying for granting his monthly retirement allowance as
well as gratuity allowance.
It is evident from Annexure-I to the supplementary affidavit
dated 27.05.2025 that, the respondent No. 2 by the memo dated
21.08.2022 categorically stated that there is no due against the
petitioner by the department and therefore, sent the relevant
documents to the concerned Ministry for granting the pension and
other ancillary retirement benefits of the petitioner.

It appears from the affidavit in opposition that an audit
objection was raised by the office of the Comptroller and Auditor
General, (C&AG), which is still unsettled. However, on meticulous
examination of the documents annexed as Annexures- 1, 1-A, 2 and
2-A of the affidavit in opposition we do not find any specific
allegation particularly against this petitioner regarding the objection

of 220 Crore Taka. It appears from Annexure-1(B) to the affidavit
in opposition that, there is an objection raised by respondent No. 2
regarding an amount of 46,550 taka against the petitioner but the
facts remains that, the respondent No. 2 has given clearance to
petitioner in granting pension, as we have already seen from
Annexure-I.
Moreover, it is evident from Annexure-3 to the affidavit-inopposition that, by the memo dated 06.12.2020 issued by the
concerned Ministry the petitioner was, in substance, exonerated
from the allegations brought against him, as the authority having
regard to the fact that he was due to retire shortly, chose to give him
only a warning without imposing any punishment under the Service
Rules of 2018.
Now it is required to examine the concerned provision of law
regarding withholding pension benefits. Section 51 of the Public
Servant Act, 2018 provides that, when a public servant goes on
retirement, if there is any departmental proceeding or any criminal
proceeding pending against him, he shall not be eligible to get the
pension benefits.
There is no other specific condition mentioned in this Act
which can disentitle an employee from having his pension benefits.

It is the clear stipulation of this Order that, if any employee is
not held personally liable by the concerned administrative

authority, was no delay shall be made in granting his pension due to
any audit objection.
In this provision though there is a time frame to resolve the
audit objection by the administrative authority, nothing has been
clearly stated as to the audit objection which remain unsettled by
the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG) neither there is any
stipulated time period mentioned within which the C&AG should
settle the audit objection. Though it has been categorically provided
that, highest preference shall be given in order to settle the audit
objection regarding an employee who is going on retirement.
It is obvious from the provision of this Order, 2020 that it
was the intention of the Government to simplify/facilitate the
pension entitlement of a retired employee and to take necessary
actions in order to disburse the pension benefits at the earliest
possible opportunity.
It is an admitted position that the petitioner retired from
service on attaining the age of superannuation in 2021. It is equally
undisputed that no department proceeding or criminal proceeding is
pending against him. The respondent no.2, who is the appointing
authority of the petitioner, has candidly stated in the affidavit in
opposition that they themselves have no claim whatsoever against
the petitioner. The Ministry also exonerated him from the
allegations by giving warning only. The sole ground put forwarded
for withholding the pension benefits of the petitioner is an unsettled
audit objection raised by the office of the C&AG.

This Court is unable to accept such contention. An audit
objection, by itself, neither determines any liability nor creates an
enforceable claim against a retired employee. Unless such objection
culminates in a finding of liability in accordance with law or forms
the basis of a duly initiated proceeding under the service rules, it
cannot be relied upon to withhold pension benefits indefinitely.
It has been already settled in many cases by this Division as
well as the Appellate Division that pension is not a matter of grace
or virtue. It is a statutory right earned by an employee in
consideration of his long and faithful service. Once the conditions
prescribed by law are fulfilled, the retired employee acquires a
corresponding right to receive his pension and other retirement
benefits without unreasonable delay. Such right cannot be defeated
by administrative inaction or by keeping an audit objection pending
for an indefinite period.
In the present case, the continued inaction of the respondents
in granting pension benefits to the petitioner is arbitrary,
unreasonable and without any lawful authority.
It is also significant that the Government itself has adopted a
policy decision requiring the authorities concerned to ensure
expeditious settlement and disbursement of pensionary benefits and
to avoid unusual delay in such matters. The impugned inaction of
the respondents is plainly inconsistent with that declared policy of
the Government and defeats it’s very objective.

A retired Government employee, having devoted his active
years to public service, is entitled to receive his retiral benefits
promptly so that he may live his post retirement life with dignity
and financial security. To compel such a person to wait indefinitely
to his lawful dues is manifestly unjust.
It transpires from the record that, during the pendency of this
Rule the learned Advocate for the petitioner by filing an application
for addition of parties made the Comptroller and Auditor General
(C&AG) as added respondent Nos. 4-7.  Though notices were
served upon them in due course, they neither appeared before this
Court nor made any submissions regarding their gross failure in
resolving the unsettled audit objection regarding the petitioner after
a long period of 5 (five) years from the date of his retirement. This
unexplained delay committed by the added respondent Nos. 4-7 is
thus found to be arbitrary and without any lawful authority.

Learned Advocate for the petitioner advanced an submission
referring to the case reported in 117 PLR 6 mentioned hereinabove
that, in that case the High Court of Punjab and Hariyana directed
the authority to grant pension benefits with 12% interest per annum
for the period of delay on the amounts to the pensioners since there
was no satisfactory explanation for the delay in disbursing the
pension benefits. Since this prayer is beyond the terms of Rule, we
are not inclined to consider this submission.
In view of the findings and discussion made hereinabove, we
therefore find merit in the instant Rule.

 Accordingly, the Rule is made absolute.
 There is no order as to cost.
 The respondents are hereby directed to disburse the lawful
entitlement of pension and service benefits to the petitioner within
30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of a copy of this judgment
and order without fail, regardless of any unsettled audit objection
before the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG). The C&AG
is also directed to resolve the pending audit objection immediately
giving highest preference to it.
Communicate the judgment and order at once.
